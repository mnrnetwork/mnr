# mnr trademark policy

**mnr**, the wordmark, the node mark and the lockups in `brand/` identify one
project: the software, the network and the service published at
**https://mnr.network**. The code is free software; the name is not.

## What "official mnr" means

There is no company behind mnr. The identity of the project is anchored in
two things the maintainers control:

1. **The domain.** Anything published at `mnr.network` and its subdomains is
   mnr. Anything else is not, whatever it calls itself.
2. **The release signing key.** Binaries and, later, the operator directory
   are signed. The key fingerprint is published at `https://mnr.network/keys`
   and in this repository once the first signed release ships. "Official"
   means "signed by that key".

## What you may do

- Refer to the project by name: "mnr", "the mnr relay", "works with mnr",
  "connects to mnr.network".
- Describe compatibility truthfully: "an mnr-compatible relayer", "implements
  the mnr protocol".
- Link to mnr.network and reproduce the mark unmodified when doing so.
- Fork the code under its licence and run your own relay or network.

## What you may not do

- Call a fork, a competing service, a package, a domain, a social account or
  a product "mnr", or anything confusingly similar (`mnr-network`, `mnrrpc`,
  `mnr.xyz`, a recoloured node mark and so on).
- Present a modified build as an official mnr release, or imply endorsement
  by the project.
- Use the mark as your own logo, or modify it.

A fork must pick its own name. It may say it is "based on mnr".

## Names we hold

The GitHub organisation `mnrnetwork`, the crates `mnr`, `mnr-core`,
`mnr-relay`, `mnr-client` and `mnr-agent`, and the domain `mnr.network`.

## Enforcement

This policy is a statement of how the maintainers use and defend the name.
Rights in the mark arise from its use, are not currently registered, and
the maintainers may register it in one or more jurisdictions at any time.
Questions and permission requests: the contact published at mnr.network.
